Why it is not the crowd

Same crowd.
Different clock.

The two peaks carry almost the same number of passengers. The morning copes. The evening does not. So the cause is not demand: it is how long the trips take against what the timetable allows.

Where the timetable is short

bus hours.
Three hours of the day.

For each route, direction and hour: the running time RTA planned, against the time that would cover 85 trips in 100. The difference, added up, is the running time the timetable does not hold. Across the whole day it is bus hours.

What a planner gets

A list.
Not a dashboard.

The routes and hours where the plan falls furthest short, and the minutes each one needs. The planner accepts or sets aside each row. Nothing applies itself.

Read from the Dataiku flow: running_time_bands, rows with 5 or more trips, ranked by extra minutes times trips. In the Dataiku web app this is the "Planner's worklist" tab, with Accept and Set aside on each row.

How it's built

Who uses it

A planner.
Every timetable change.

RTA changes timetables every one to two months and routes four times a year. Each night adds a day of records. Before each change, a planner opens the worklist, sees where the evening runs long, and decides how much time to give it. Boardings, never people "waiting". Nothing about one card, one driver or one vehicle is shown.

its time.
