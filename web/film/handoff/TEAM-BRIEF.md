# Five-person stage briefing

This is a simulated incident played by consenting team members. Brief everyone before dialling. The AI uses natural operational language and identifies itself as Insiyab; it does not repeat a disclaimer. The dashboard keeps the demonstration label visible.

Each person answers in their own words. These role cards are invented conditions, not facts from the RTA dataset.

| Role | What you know in this scenario | What to tell the agent |
|---|---|---|
| Driver | The bus can continue but will return late. | “We can continue. Plan for a return about twenty minutes late.” |
| Depot controller | One spare could be prepared, subject to checks. | “We have one spare. Allow fifteen minutes, and I still need to confirm the driver and vehicle checks.” |
| Station supervisor | The departure display needs a revised time. | “We need an updated departure time for passengers. I can update the display once the manager decides.” |
| Planning analyst | The recorded-day analysis supports reviewing the evening timetable. | “The times come from the recorded day. They are not a live forecast, and they do not prove a spare is available.” |
| Duty manager | You hear the reports, including anything missing, then choose. | Choose one offered option in plain words. If information is insufficient, choose to watch and take no action. |

On the callback, confirm that you received the manager’s message. If you disagree or have new information, say so; the agent must not invent your acknowledgement. No actual vehicle is dispatched by this system.

Before starting: phones available, incoming calls enabled, everyone knows their role, shared screen connected with the private viewing key. During the run: keep the phone near the microphone only if the presenter wants the room to hear it; avoid speaker feedback. Afterward: check all reports, the recorded choice and every acknowledgement, rather than judging success by ringing alone.
