Insiyab · the second clock

And on the day itself.

A planner can give the evening its time before the day. On the day, one thing can still be seen coming: a bus that is not back yet when its next trip is due to leave.

This page describes a drill on the team's own phones. No RTA system was contacted, and no call is placed from this page. The figures are totals from one real day, Monday 15 December 2025.

The signal: the bus is still out

696departures where the bus was still on its previous trip at the time it was due to leave
85%of those then left more than 5 minutes late; the middle one left 13 minutes late
110routes and 377 buses had this happen at least once
62%of all departures more than 5 minutes late (589 of 945) had the bus back after the due time

Counted over the 9,787 trips whose bus ran an earlier trip that day with a planned break of up to two hours.

It is an evening signal

Departures per hour with the bus still out. At 19:00 it is 140 of 651 departures, about one in five. Before 16:00 it is about one in thirty.

Earlier warning means more false flags. Five minutes before is the useful point: about three flags in five turn into a late departure. This rule is measured, not forecast: the running-time model adds nothing to it at 15 to 60 minutes ahead, and we say so.

One message to the control room

To: duty manager, operations control centre · drill

Route 310: the 19:23 departure will leave late

The bus for this trip is still on its previous trip and is overdue. Expected to leave about 13 minutes late. The planned break was 16 minutes and is used up.

Choose one:

Report. Decide. Relay.

1. Report

Three phones ring at once. Each person gives one line.

  • Driver: where the bus is and when it will be back.
  • Depot: whether a spare bus and driver are ready.
  • Station: how many people are waiting.

2. Decide

The duty manager's phone rings with the three reports and the three options. A person chooses. The tool does not.

3. Relay

The same three people are rung back with the decision, and it is logged with the time.

Choose an option in the message above to see what is passed on.

What this is not

  • It is not emergency or incident handling. The day's data has no record of breakdowns, closures or their causes; it shows delay carried from one trip to the next.
  • It is one day, a Monday in the school holidays.
  • The example wording, the 6 minute hold and the three options are illustrations for the drill, not RTA procedure.
  • The phone calls are a demonstration to the team's own phones.

Source: RTA running-time data for 15 December 2025, read inside Dataiku; only totals leave it. Method: trips linked to the previous trip of the same bus; a departure counts as "bus still out" when that previous trip arrived after the departure was due.
