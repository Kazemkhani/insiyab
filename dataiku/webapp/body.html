How it is built

The Dataiku Flow has three processing zones. Each zone is a set of recipes that read raw data, clean it, and produce the aggregated datasets this webapp reads.

Zone: Service reliability

Reads RTA's automated vehicle location (AVL) feed for 15 December 2025. Aligns each actual departure to its scheduled time, computes the difference, and aggregates it three ways.

Python prepare_avl: parses dates in the form 15DEC2025, falls back to the nearest same weekday when a timetable feed covers a different season, and exports the community map geometry to the column format the engine reads.
Group punctuality_by_hour: aggregates departure lateness by service-day hour (3 to 30; hours 24–30 are after midnight). Dataset read by Tab 1 and Tab 2.
Group punctuality_by_route_hour: same aggregation split by route. About 3,200 rows. Dataset read by Tab 2.
Group punctuality_by_bus_type: aggregation split by vehicle type (STANDARD, DOUBLE, ARTICULATED, MIDI, COACH). Dataset read by Tab 2.

Zone: Passenger demand

Reads RTA's nol card transaction records for the same day. Each tap-on and tap-off becomes a boarding or alighting event, aggregated by hour, by route and hour, and by stop.

Group demand_by_hour: total boardings and alightings by service-day hour. Dataset read by Tab 1 and Tab 3.
Group demand_by_route_hour: same, split by route. About 3,700 rows. Dataset read by Tab 3.
Group demand_by_stop: boardings and alightings by stop. About 2,600 rows. Dataset read by Tab 3 (top 15 busiest).

Zone: Supply and capacity

Joins departure counts and demand figures. Applies RTA's planning rule: passengers boarding divided by seats per bus gives buses needed. It classifies each route-hour by status.

Python join_supply_demand: joins punctuality (trips run, seats) with demand (boardings) on route and hour. Routes not found in the running-time table receive status "route not in the running-time table".
Group supply_vs_demand_by_route_hour: full route-hour table with buses needed, buses short, and status. Dataset read by the Supply and capacity tab and the What-if tab.
Group supply_status_by_hour: count of route-hours in each status, by hour. Dataset read by the Supply and capacity tab.

RTA facts that make this usable

These were told to the team by RTA's scheduling engineer:

  • Timetables change about every one to two months. The route network changes four times a year, because changes must be published to stops and journey planners. The Flow is sized to re-run when a new timetable feed arrives.
  • Spare buses are sent from staffed stations when the next bus is full and none follows within the route's frequency. This webapp names where that is happening, not how often it succeeds.
  • Most stops are not monitored with physical sensors. "Boardings" here means nol card tap-ons, not all passengers.
