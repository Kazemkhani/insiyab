How it is built

The Flow in Dataiku has four zones, one for each question this app answers, with the same names as the tabs. Eight of its fourteen steps are visual Group recipes; six are short Python recipes, each opening with a plain description of what it does. Every figure in this app is read live from the tables those steps write.

Zone: Passenger demand

The question: how many people board, where and when? Starts from RTA's nol taps for Monday 15 December 2025.

Python count_taps: reads the taps. A check-in is a boarding and a check-out an alighting. Taps before 03:00 belong to the end of the service day, so they count as hours 24 to 26. The card, the device and the vehicle are dropped here: the output, taps_by_route_stop_hour, holds counts only.
Group demand_by_hour: boardings and alightings added up by hour. Read by The day and Passenger demand.
Group demand_by_route_hour: the same by route and hour. Read by Passenger demand, Supply and capacity and the forecast.
Group demand_by_stop: the same by stop. Read by Passenger demand.

Zone: Service reliability

The question: does the bus leave on time? Starts from RTA's running-time table and the fleet list.

Python clean_running_time: one row per bus per stop. Turns clock text into seconds on the service-day clock (after midnight is written 24:00 to 30:59), works out how late each departure was against its own scheduled time, and adds the bus type. The driver and the vehicle number are dropped here. Output: stop_departures_clean.
Group punctuality_by_hour: share of departures 5 or more minutes late, average lateness, share leaving early and share of stops passed without opening the doors, by hour. Read by The day.
Group punctuality_by_route_hour: the same by route and hour. Read by Service reliability.
Group punctuality_by_bus_type: the same by bus type. Read by Service reliability.

Zone: Supply and capacity

The question: were enough buses sent? Uses the results of the two zones above.

Python buses_needed_rule: applies RTA's planning rule as its scheduling engineer described it: boardings in the hour, divided by what one bus carries, gives the buses needed. Counts the trips that started on each route in each hour and their seats, joins the boardings, and works the rule twice: seats only, and seats plus standing (the fleet list marks standing as not verified). A route with taps but no trip in the running-time table is marked, not counted as short. Output: supply_vs_demand_by_route_hour. Read by Supply and capacity and What if.
Group supply_status_by_hour: how many routes were short, in balance or under a fifth used, by hour. Read by Supply and capacity.

Zone: Forecast

The question: how long will each trip really take? Uses the running-time table, the fleet list and the boardings from Passenger demand.

Python build_trip_table: one row per trip, holding only what was knowable 30 minutes before it was due to leave: the planned running time, the stops, the time of day, the bus type, how the route ran in the two hours before (finished trips only), how late this bus finished its previous trip (only if it had finished), and boardings on the route in the last full hour. The vehicle number links a bus to its previous trip and is then dropped.
Python forecast_running_time: gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM). Learns from trips due before 16:00 and is tested on every trip from 16:00 on. Scored against RTA's timetable and two simple forecasts, with the average miss, RMSE and MAPE, and a range for the gain from drawing whole routes again 200 times. Outputs: running_time_forecast, running_time_metrics, running_time_drivers.
Python running_time_bands: for each route, direction and hour: the planned running time, the middle trip, and the time that covers 85 trips in 100. The difference is the extra time that hour needs.
Group running_time_ledger_by_hour: those extra minutes added up by hour of the day.

RTA facts that inform this analysis

These were told to the team by RTA's scheduling engineer:

  • Timetables change about every one to two months. The route network changes four times a year. The Flow is sized to re-run when a new timetable feed arrives.
  • Spare buses are sent from staffed stations when the next bus is full and none follows within the route's frequency. This webapp names where that is happening, not how often it succeeds.
  • Most stops are not monitored with physical sensors. "Boardings" here means nol card tap-ons, not all passengers.
  • Standing capacity in the fleet register is marked not verified. RTA plans with about 35 standing places per standard bus.
