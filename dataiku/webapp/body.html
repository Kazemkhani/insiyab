How this runs

  • What goes in each night: the day's nol card taps and vehicle running times; nothing new to measure, only new records.
  • What is rebuilt: the Flow's zones, by a nightly scenario in Dataiku; each zone re-reads all data to date.
  • What comes out: this list and the forecast it rests on, ready for the next timetable review.

What it does not do: it does not change a route, it does not act by itself, and it rests on one day's data until more days are added.

Step 5 of 8 · Flow zone: Supply and capacity

… routes short at 18:00,
by RTA's own rule of thumb.

from supply_status_by_hour

The detail

RTA's planning rule: passengers boarding in the hour divided by what one bus carries gives the buses needed. Buses short = buses needed minus buses that ran. The rule counts every boarding against a seat so it leans high; a standing-room figure would show fewer short. The same rule finds a similar shortfall in the morning peak too; the morning copes because timetable slack absorbs it.

The detail

Zone 1: Passenger demand

The question: how many people board, where and when? Starts from RTA's nol taps for Monday 15 December 2025.

Python count_taps: reads the taps. A check-in is a boarding and a check-out an alighting. Taps before 03:00 belong to the end of the service day, so they count as hours 24 to 26. The card, the device and the vehicle are dropped here: the output holds counts only.
Group demand_by_hour: boardings and alightings added up by hour.
Group demand_by_route_hour: the same by route and hour.
Group demand_by_stop: the same by stop.

Zone 2: Service reliability

The question: does the bus leave on time? Starts from RTA's running-time table and the fleet list.

Python clean_running_time: one row per bus per stop. Turns clock text into seconds on the service-day clock, works out how late each departure was, and adds the bus type. The driver and the vehicle number are dropped here.
Group punctuality_by_hour: share of departures 5 or more minutes late, average lateness, share leaving early and share of stops passed without opening the doors, by hour.
Group punctuality_by_route_hour: the same by route and hour.
Group punctuality_by_bus_type: the same by bus type.

Zone 3: Supply and capacity

The question: were enough buses sent? Uses the results of Zones 1 and 2.

Python buses_needed_rule: applies RTA's planning rule as its scheduling engineer described it: boardings in the hour, divided by what one bus carries, gives the buses needed. Output: supply_vs_demand_by_route_hour.
Group supply_status_by_hour: how many routes were short, in balance or under-used, by hour.

Zone 4: Forecast

The question: how long will each trip really take? Uses the running-time table, the fleet list and boardings from Zone 1.

Python build_trip_table: one row per trip, holding only what was knowable 30 minutes before it was due to leave: planned running time, time of day, bus type, how the route ran in the two hours before, and boardings on the route in the last full hour. The vehicle number links a bus to its previous trip and is then dropped.
Python forecast_running_time: gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM). Learns from trips due before 16:00 and is tested on every trip from 16:00 on. Outputs: running_time_forecast, running_time_metrics, running_time_drivers.
Python running_time_bands: for each route, direction and hour: the planned running time, the middle trip, and the time that covers 85 trips in 100. The difference is the extra time that hour needs.
Group running_time_ledger_by_hour: those extra minutes added up by hour of the day.
