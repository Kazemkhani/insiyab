How it is built

The Dataiku Flow has four processing zones. Each zone is a set of recipes that read raw data, clean it, and produce the aggregated datasets this webapp reads.

Zone: Passenger demand

Reads RTA's nol card transaction records for 15 December 2025. Each tap-on and tap-off becomes a boarding or alighting event, placed at the nearest stop on the matching trip, then aggregated.

Python count_taps: matches each nol tap to a trip using vehicle number and time; handles door-open (boarding) and drive-through (count) tap types; places taps at the stop that best fits time and direction.
Group demand_by_hour: total boardings and alightings by service-day hour. Read by The day tab and the Passenger demand tab.
Group demand_by_route_hour: split by route. About 3,700 rows. Read by the Passenger demand tab.
Group demand_by_stop: boardings and alightings by stop. About 2,600 rows. Read by the Passenger demand tab (top 15).

Zone: Service reliability

Reads RTA's automated vehicle location (AVL) feed. Aligns each actual departure to its scheduled time, computes the lateness, and aggregates it three ways.

Python clean_running_time: parses two-digit years, times without seconds, and service-day hours past 24:00; rejects trips with implausible running times; outputs one row per departure.
Group punctuality_by_hour: aggregates departure lateness by service-day hour (3 to 30; hours 24-30 are after midnight). Read by The day tab and the Service reliability tab.
Group punctuality_by_route_hour: same split by route. About 3,200 rows. Read by the Service reliability tab.
Group punctuality_by_bus_type: split by vehicle type (STANDARD, DOUBLE, ARTICULATED, MIDI, COACH). Read by the Service reliability tab.

Zone: Supply and capacity

Joins departure counts and demand figures. Applies RTA's planning rule: passengers boarding divided by seats per bus gives buses needed, and classifies each route-hour by status.

Python buses_needed_rule: joins punctuality (trips run, seats per bus, standing places) with demand (boardings) on route and hour. Routes not in the running-time table are flagged. Computes buses needed under both the seated rule and the standing rule (places per bus from the fleet register, marked not verified).
Group supply_vs_demand_by_route_hour: full route-hour table with buses needed, buses short, and status. Read by the Supply and capacity tab and the What-if tab.
Group supply_status_by_hour: count of route-hours in each status, by hour. Read by the Supply and capacity tab.

Zone: Forecast

Builds a gradient-boosted tree model that predicts how long a trip will take. Tested on the evening peak and compared against the timetable and simple baselines.

Python build_trip_table: joins one row per trip from AVL, demand, timetable, and fleet register; adds lag features (how late the previous trip on this bus finished, what boardings were in the last full hour on this route).
Python forecast_running_time: trains a gradient-boosted tree on trips before 16:00, scores trips from 16:00 to end of service. Outputs trip-level forecasts plus feature importances.
Python running_time_bands (optional): groups each route-hour into percentile bands (median, 85th percentile) to show planners how much buffer is needed. Built separately; the Forecast tab shows a note if this dataset is absent.

RTA facts that inform this analysis

These were told to the team by RTA's scheduling engineer:

  • Timetables change about every one to two months. The route network changes four times a year. The Flow is sized to re-run when a new timetable feed arrives.
  • Spare buses are sent from staffed stations when the next bus is full and none follows within the route's frequency. This webapp names where that is happening, not how often it succeeds.
  • Most stops are not monitored with physical sensors. "Boardings" here means nol card tap-ons, not all passengers.
  • Standing capacity in the fleet register is marked not verified. RTA plans with about 35 standing places per standard bus.
