# Survey programme: opt-in micro-surveys funded by vendor partners

Written by lane 1, 18 November 2026, after judge feedback on the same morning.

**This is a proposal.** No uptake has been measured, no vendor has been approached, and no survey has been run. Every figure in this document describing passenger uptake or vendor benefit is a projection or an assumption, and is labelled as such.

---

## Why surveys

The judges identified a gap: Insiyab's forecasts are only as good as their data. Trip purpose, transfer patterns, event intent and destination are not in the nol tap data. These signal where demand will grow, which small events need a standby bus, and whether a disruption is pushing passengers to other modes. Opt-in micro-surveys, funded by vendor partners who receive aggregate insight in return, provide this data without tracking individuals.

The judges also said the restaurant-reward concept lacked a clear return on investment for the brand. The programme below answers that: each vendor type receives concrete aggregate insight tailored to their planning decisions, not a generalised mention that their vouchers were redeemed.

---

## Three survey moments

Surveys are offered at moments when a passenger has a reason to respond and a few seconds to spare. Each survey is three questions and takes under 30 seconds.

| Moment | When it fires | Why the passenger responds |
|---|---|---|
| After a disruption | A bus ran more than 10 minutes late, or a service was cancelled; the passenger's nol card recorded a tap on that route and hour | The passenger has just been affected; a reward for 30 seconds feels fair |
| Before an event | A passenger boards a route that serves a known venue in the next 48 hours, and an event is scheduled there | The passenger is likely en route; the survey confirms intent and helps the planner |
| At a transfer | A passenger checks out at a major interchange and checks in again within 15 minutes | The transfer is recorded; the survey adds the destination and purpose the tap does not carry |

---

## The three questions

Every survey uses the same three-question structure, adapted to the moment:

1. **Where are you going?** (a list of broad destination types: work, home, school or university, event or entertainment, shopping, medical, other)
2. **How often do you use this route?** (daily, a few times a week, once a week, less often, first time)
3. **Which reward would you value most today?** (a short list of options funded by the partner for that survey)

Question 3 doubles as the reward selection. No question asks for a name, card number, phone number or any personal identifier. The tap-in time and route are already known to RTA; the survey adds no new identifier.

## Vendor types, rewards, questions and returns

A vendor sponsors one survey moment per campaign. In exchange they receive the aggregate responses from that moment for the duration of the campaign. No individual passenger's answer, card, device or trip is shared with any vendor at any time.

| Vendor type | Example in Dubai | Reward funded | The three questions in this context | Aggregate insight the vendor receives (their return on investment) | What RTA receives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Privacy: what is never shared

- No individual passenger's answer is linked to their nol card number, device, name or trip record outside RTA's own systems.
- Vendors receive only aggregated, anonymised totals: for example, "340 passengers travelling to work answered this survey; 28% preferred a data-plan reward."
- No survey result is broken down to fewer than 20 respondents. Groups smaller than that are suppressed.
- Participation is opt-in at every survey. A passenger who declines sees no further requests on that journey.
- No vendor may use the data to identify, target or exclude an individual passenger.
- RTA retains and controls the data. Vendors receive a summary report, not a data extract.

---

## What this adds to the forecast

Survey data is not used in the current trip-time or boardings forecast: the model runs on the AVL report and nol taps alone. The survey programme is the proposed next data layer: it would add trip purpose and event intent as features for the event-planning module, and would supply the destination data needed to route passengers to alternative services after a disruption.
